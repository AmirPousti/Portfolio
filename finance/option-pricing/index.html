Assumptions, formulas and source reconciliation

Starting point. The March 2026 group report’s one-step example uses spot and strike of 6,466.58, 9.6278% annual volatility, a 3.9984% annual risk-free rate and six months to expiry, dated 13 August 2025. Its full-sample volatility of 18.57% belongs to a different estimation period.

One consistent convention. For each step Δt = T / n: u = exp(σ√Δt), d = 1/u, R = exp(rΔt) and q = (R − d)/(u − d). At expiry C = max(S − K, 0); at earlier nodes C = [qCup + (1 − q)Cdown]/R. Rates are continuously compounded throughout, with constant volatility and no dividends.

Reconciliation. The report mixes an effective-annual probability formula with continuous discounting, and its printed probability labels disagree. Continuous compounding throughout reproduces 281.8541, rounded to the reported 281.85. The explorer recalculates the probabilities and every node instead of copying those inconsistent labels.

Two-step boundary. Switching to two steps keeps the same dated inputs. It does not reproduce the report’s later 252.20 and 256.59 examples, which use different, partly ex-post volatility and rate estimates. One or two steps illustrate the mechanics; they are a coarse approximation, not a calibrated market option quote. Values are index points with no contract multiplier.

Decisions and lessons · Group project

Keep the information date in view.

Decision
The report compared simple CRR trees with a later time-varying example to examine how volatility and rates affect the result.
Lesson
A model fitted with information realised after issuance answers a different question from a price formed on the issuance date. A more elaborate tree does not remove that distinction.
Next check
A further study would estimate every input using only information available at issuance, use one rate convention throughout and test convergence with more time steps.
