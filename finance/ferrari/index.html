Investment view

Exceptional franchise quality, but limited margin of safety.

The report initiated coverage with a HOLD recommendation and a €325.32 target price, only 2.03% above the €318.85 reference price. Ferrari’s controlled-supply model continued to produce pricing power, strong margins and cash generation; however, the narrow valuation gap suggested that much of this quality was already reflected in the share price.

Business quality

Growth came from value per car, not more cars.

FY2025 shipments declined by 0.8% to 13,640 units, while group revenue increased by 7.0% to €7.15bn. Cars-and-spares revenue per shipment rose by 5.7% to approximately €440k, reflecting pricing, model mix and personalisation rather than volume expansion.

Customer behaviour reinforced the scarcity thesis: approximately 84% of new cars went to existing owners and 56% went to clients who already owned more than one Ferrari.

Forecast outlook

Pricing, mix and personalisation carry the model.

The base case assumed shipment growth of only 0.5% per year. Cars-and-spares revenue growth began at 4.5% in FY2026F, peaked at 5.5% in FY2027–28F and faded to 2.5% by FY2035F. The model therefore required most growth to come from higher value per vehicle rather than higher production.

Assumptions, calculation & source notes

Each FY2026–35 cash flow is discounted by (1 + WACC)year − 181/365. Terminal value is FY2035 cash flow × (1 + growth) ÷ (WACC − growth), discounted over 10 − 181/365 years. WACC must exceed terminal growth.

The equity bridge applies −€9.679m net debt (31 December 2025) and −€7.849m non-controlling interests to enterprise value, then divides by 175,916,530 shares. All operating forecasts, the equity bridge and share count are held fixed.

Catalysts and risks

Catalysts

  • Further pricing and personalisation gains.
  • Successful launches, including Ferrari Luce deliveries from late 2026.
  • Higher dividends and continued share repurchases.

Key risks

  • Weaker pricing or mix with limited support from volume.
  • Electrification costs or weaker demand for an electric Ferrari.
  • Currency, tariffs, regulation and valuation sensitivity.

Decisions and lessons · Individual research

Separate the business case from the price.

My contribution
I built the 24-sheet operating and valuation model and developed the investment thesis, forecast assumptions and original HOLD conclusion.
Decision
The business’s pricing power supported the franchise thesis. At the dated reference price, the blended valuation offered only 2.03% upside, so the recommendation remained restrained.
Lesson
With roughly 75% of DCF enterprise value in the terminal period, assumptions about discount rates and long-run growth deserve as much attention as the headline price. The explorer makes that sensitivity visible.
Next check
A future update would revisit operating assumptions and the dated market inputs, then recalculate and reconcile every valuation output.

Recommendation

A high-quality business was not automatically an attractive security.

Ferrari’s scarcity, loyalty, margins and cash generation supported the investment case. At the stated price, however, the original valuation offered only modest upside. The report therefore concluded with a HOLD: a positive view of the franchise, but a restrained view of the risk-reward balance.
